# NIST CSF 2.0 — Core Concepts

## **1. CSF Core**

**Definition:** The CSF Core is a structured set of cybersecurity outcomes organized into Functions, Categories, and Subcategories. It helps organizations understand, assess, and manage cybersecurity risks.

**Components:**
- **Functions:** High-level cybersecurity outcomes.
- **Categories:** Groups of related cybersecurity outcomes.
- **Subcategories:** More specific outcomes within Categories.

**Key Takeaway:** The CSF Core explains what cybersecurity outcomes an organization should work toward, not one mandatory way to achieve them.

## **2. CSF Profiles**

**Definition:** CSF Profiles help an organization describe and prioritize its cybersecurity posture based on its objectives, risks, requirements, and resources.

### **2.1 Current Profile**
Describes the cybersecurity outcomes the organization currently achieves or is attempting to achieve.

### **2.2 Target Profile**
Describes the cybersecurity outcomes the organization wants to achieve to meet its risk management objectives.

### **2.3 Community Profile**
Provides shared cybersecurity outcomes and guidance for organizations with common needs, such as a sector, technology, or threat.

**Example:** Comparing a Current Profile with a Target Profile helps identify security gaps and prioritize improvements.

**Key Takeaway:** Profiles help organizations understand their current security posture, define their desired posture, and plan improvements.

## **3. CSF Tiers**

**Definition:** CSF Tiers characterize the rigor of an organization's cybersecurity risk governance and management practices.

### **3.1 Tier 1 — Partial**
Cybersecurity risk management practices are largely informal and reactive.

### **3.2 Tier 2 — Risk Informed**
Risk management practices are informed by organizational objectives and risk awareness, but may not be consistently organization-wide.

### **3.3 Tier 3 — Repeatable**
Cybersecurity risk management practices are formally established, approved, and applied consistently.

### **3.4 Tier 4 — Adaptive**
Cybersecurity practices adapt continuously using lessons learned, changing threats, and predictive indicators.

**Key Takeaway:** Tiers describe how cybersecurity risks are governed and managed. They are not automatically equivalent to certification levels or maturity scores.

## **4. Categories and Subcategories**

### **4.1 Categories**
**Definition:** Categories group related cybersecurity outcomes within a Function.

**Example:** Under Protect, **Identity Management, Authentication, and Access Control (PR.AA)** is a Category.

### **4.2 Subcategories**
**Definition:** Subcategories describe more specific cybersecurity outcomes within a Category.

**Example:** **PR.AA-01** addresses the management of identities and credentials for authorized users, services, and hardware.

**Key Takeaway:** Categories organize related security outcomes; Subcategories provide more specific outcomes to assess and address.

## **5. Functions**

**Definition:** Functions are the highest-level groupings of cybersecurity outcomes in the CSF Core. CSF 2.0 has six Functions.

### **5.1 Govern (GV)**
Establish and monitor cybersecurity risk management strategy, expectations, policies, and responsibilities.

### **5.2 Identify (ID)**
Understand organizational assets, cybersecurity risks, and opportunities for improvement.

### **5.3 Protect (PR)**
Use safeguards to manage cybersecurity risks.

### **5.4 Detect (DE)**
Find and analyze possible cybersecurity attacks and compromises.

### **5.5 Respond (RS)**
Take action regarding detected cybersecurity incidents.

### **5.6 Recover (RC)**
Restore assets and operations affected by cybersecurity incidents.

**Key Takeaway:** Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover organize cybersecurity risk management from oversight and prevention through incident handling and restoration.

## **6. How the Concepts Fit Together**

- **Functions** organize the highest-level cybersecurity outcomes.
- **Categories** group related outcomes within each Function.
- **Subcategories** describe more specific outcomes within each Category.
- **Profiles** help organizations select and compare outcomes for their current and target security posture.
- **Tiers** characterize the rigor of their cybersecurity risk governance and management practices.
