# 🚦 SOC Severity Levels & Prioritization

## Definition
SOC Severity Levels classify security alerts and incidents based on their potential impact and seriousness. Prioritization helps analysts decide which issues must be investigated and handled first.

## Examples
- **Critical:** Active ransomware attack affecting important business systems.
- **High:** Confirmed unauthorized access to a privileged account.
- **Medium:** Suspicious login activity that requires further investigation.
- **Low:** A minor security policy violation with limited impact.

## Characteristics
- **Critical:** Severe impact; requires immediate response and escalation.
- **High:** Significant security risk; requires urgent investigation.
- **Medium:** Moderate risk; should be investigated within the defined response time.
- **Low:** Limited risk; can usually be handled through routine investigation.
- **Prioritization:** Helps SOC teams allocate resources based on urgency.
- **Escalation:** Serious incidents are forwarded to higher-level analysts or incident response teams.

## Important Details

| Severity | Example | Typical Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Active ransomware spreading across systems | Immediate |
| High | Confirmed compromise of an administrator account | Urgent |
| Medium | Suspicious login with no confirmed compromise | Normal |
| Low | Minor policy violation | Routine |

**Severity vs. Priority**
- **Severity** describes how serious the security impact is.
- **Priority** describes how quickly the issue should be handled.

Priority can also depend on business importance, affected systems, and whether an attack is actively occurring. Exact severity definitions and response times vary between organizations.

## Key Takeaway
Severity levels help SOC analysts understand the seriousness of security incidents, while prioritization ensures the most urgent threats receive attention first.
